# Finding events

## Goal
Find upcoming events (next 3–9 months) where GeneralMind can meet its buyers face to face, and the public pages that name the people and companies there.

## How we meet people
We can't cold email (in Germany it needs prior consent, between businesses too), so there are no invitation campaigns. Speakers are met in the break after their talk; exhibitors who are prospects are met at their stands. A few of the very best can be booked through the event's own app where it allows it, or through an introduction. So what matters is who is there, when they're on stage, and whether we can get in.

## Who we're looking for
Heads of procurement, supply chain, logistics, order management / customer service (order-to-cash), finance / accounts payable (procure-to-pay) and operations, at mid-to-large manufacturers, wholesalers and distributors, retailers, consumer brands and logistics firms. DACH first; pan-European events are fine when DACH companies attend. Running SAP, Oracle, JD Edwards or Microsoft Dynamics is a plus.

## Where to look
- Procurement congresses (BME and its events, Handelsblatt / Euroforum, Procurement Summit)
- Supply chain and logistics (BVL, EHI, manufacturing and supply chain summits, LogiMAT, transport logistic)
- SAP ecosystem (DSAG events, SAP finance and spend events)
- Finance, accounts payable and e-invoicing (shared services weeks, E-Rechnungs-Gipfel)
- Industry events for our customer types: metals and steel distribution, food and beverage, wholesale, retail, manufacturing (Hannover Messe)

## How to judge an event
1. Who we could actually meet: practitioners from buyer companies on stage, or exhibitors who are themselves buyers (manufacturers at Hannover Messe, logistics firms at logistics fairs). Exhibitors at most congresses are vendors: useful for competitor mapping, not as leads.
2. Access: can a software vendor attend, and how? Note whether it's open, invitation only, or vendors only as paying partners. Invitation-only and sponsor-only events are listed and marked, but look for an alternative that passes.
3. Cost per meeting: the vendor ticket, travel and hotel for the people we'd send, against the conversations we'd get. A dear ticket can be worth it if the list is strong.
4. Names and times available: a public speaker list, agenda, exhibitor or participant list, and ideally the timetable (session times, rooms, breaks).
5. Timing: soon enough to act on, far enough to plan.
6. DACH relevance.
7. Bonus: GeneralMind is a partner there (exhibiting, speaking or pitching).

## Record for each event
Format, dates and venue, access (open, invitation only, vendors only as partners), the vendor ticket price excluding VAT (with the price tier and date), where the prospects are (on stage, at the stands, or both), and whether this year's timetable is out.

## Rules
- Confirm dates, access and prices on the organiser's own site, never an aggregator alone. Mark anything unconfirmed.
- If this year's lists aren't out, use the previous edition's and label it.
- Skip events that have already happened.
